## 📖 Narrative Design


### Story Synopsis

After a violent, unnatural tempest shatters the coastal settlements of the Panay-adjacent archipelago, a nameless villager wakes upon the salt-scoured shore with no memory of their lineage or the storm's origin. Amid shattered barangay structures and terrified survivors, the player must forage, farm, and engage in delicate barter trade to rebuild the ruined village while unearthing lost oral histories. As the fog clears, strange bioluminescent flora and silent spirit-whispers reveal that the catastrophe was no ordinary monsoon, but a spiritual fracture caused by a broken ancestral oath. To save their people from permanent unraveling, the player must navigate factional divides, fulfill traditional rituals, and weave together the fragmented memories of the living and the dead.


### Core Themes

- Collective memory as survival and identity
- Grief and communal resilience in times of crisis
- Animism and human reciprocity with nature
- The fragility of social oaths and ancestral legacy


### 🌀 The Storm — Hidden Truth

> The storm was not a natural disaster nor divine punishment, but the violent resonance of a shattered 'Lagda'—a sacred ancestral copper plate covenant that bound the cosmic hunger of the Bakunawa (the celestial serpent) beneath the trench. Generations ago, the island's founders sealed the creature using their unified memories as living anchors. As modern barangays drifted into factional greed and forgotten rituals, the oath dissolved. The tempest was the serpent's breath leaking through the ruptured seal, consuming the islanders' shared memories to fuel its rebirth. The player's lost memory is the direct result of carrying the final shard of the ancestral oath.


### Characters


#### Datu Kalaw — *Chieftain of the Coastal Barangay*
A battle-weary leader who lost his eldest son and half his fleet during the night of the tempest. He struggles to maintain authority through traditional law while famine threatens his people.
- **Motivation:** To restore order, feed his people through trade and agriculture, and prevent his barangay from fracturing into warring bands.
- **Relationship to Player:** Acts as a stern mentor and authority figure who grants land rights in exchange for the player's assistance in village rebuilding.


#### Babaylan Alunsina — *Elder Shaman and Spirit Medium*
An revered herbalist and ritualist whose connection to the Anito (ancestral spirits) was severed when the storm struck, leaving her hearing only agonizing static from the spirit world.
- **Motivation:** To mend the spiritual tether between the living and the dead before corrupt Anito transform into hostile Aswang.
- **Relationship to Player:** Recognizes the player's unique spiritual emptiness as a vessel capable of holding lost memories and guides their ritual journeys.


#### Limbas — *Outcast Navigator and Seafaring Trader*
A skilled Karakoa captain from a neighboring islet who survived the storm at sea. He possesses rare trade goods but refuses to settle in any permanent village.
- **Motivation:** To barter exotic materials, map the altered sea currents, and search for his missing crew.
- **Relationship to Player:** A pragmatic trade partner who provides access to foreign goods and deep-sea fishing grounds in exchange for rare forged commodities.


#### Dayang Mayari — *Artisan and Lore-Keeper*
A master weaver and goldsmith who lost her entire workshop during the deluge. She is one of the few who can still read and etch the ancient Surat script on bamboo tags.
- **Motivation:** To record every survivor's story before their memories fade into the storm's mist.
- **Relationship to Player:** A compassionate confidante who crafts key narrative items and unlocks lore entries as the player brings her recovered artifacts.


#### Bagani Baya — *War Band Commander*
A former champion warrior who believes Datu Kalaw's diplomatic barter system is too weak for post-disaster survival. He advocates for armed expansion and resource seizure.
- **Motivation:** To secure the island's remaining fertile highland terraces by force and purge foreign influences.
- **Relationship to Player:** A volatile rival who tests the player's resolve, offering martial rewards if the player aligns with his militaristic vision.


#### Ubag — *Orphaned Scavenger Child*
A silent child found wandering near the ancient Balete tree after the storm, completely unharmed yet unable to speak spoken words.
- **Motivation:** To collect shiny trinkets, sea glass, and copper scraps to build miniature shrines across the island.
- **Relationship to Player:** A mysterious companion who leads the player to hidden foraging nodes and secret Anito altars.


### Factions


#### Kadatuan sa Baybay (Coastal Council) (Lawful Neutral)
The traditional ruling class composed of chieftains, elders, and prominent maritime traders focused on civil order, barter trade networks, and agricultural recovery.
- **Key NPCs:** Datu Kalaw, Dayang Mayari


#### Circle of the Anito (Neutral Good)
A secluded fellowship of Babaylan mediums, herbalists, and spiritual devotees dedicated to appeasing enraged nature spirits and performing funerary rites for the unburied.
- **Key NPCs:** Babaylan Alunsina, Ubag


#### Mga Pahimatngon (The Silent Guard) (Chaotic Neutral)
A radical isolationist military order formed by traumatized warriors who believe foreign trade and broken taboos summoned the storm. They seek to seal the island from all outsiders.
- **Key NPCs:** Bagani Baya


### Main Quest Arc

**Ang Handumanan sa Dawa (The Memory of the Dawn)**

The player must rebuild the destroyed barangay while gathering four shattered fragments of the Copper Lagda scattered across the island. By restoring trade routes, cultivating sacred crops, and conducting Anito rituals, the player gradually pieces together their own identity and learns how to reseal the Bakunawa's trench before the second tempest swallows the archipelago whole.

**Objectives:**
1. Rebuild the central Barangay Longhouse and establish basic crop farming
2. Establish trade alliances between the Kadatuan and Limbas's seafaring fleet
3. Recover the three broken copper fragments from flooded caves and ancient burial sites
4. Perform the Sandugo (Blood Compact Ritual) to unite the divided factions
5. Descend into the Submerged Abyss to re-etch the sacred covenant upon the ocean bedrock

**Reward:** Restoration of the archipelago's climate, unlocking the title of 'Handi-Labi' (Master Lore-Keeper), and securing the permanent survival of the unified barangay.

### Lore Entries


#### The First Oath and the Copper Lagda
*Before the coming of iron or gold, the seven paramount chieftains met upon the white sands of Panay to forge a pact with the sky and sea. They etched their promises upon a plate of beaten copper—a Lagda—pledging that no barangay would hoard the fruits of the land while another starved. So long as barter remained fair and honor was kept in the Sandugo, the sky deities promised gentle rains and calm waters.*


#### On the Nature of Anito and Aswang
*The Anito are not distant gods, but our own grandfathers, mothers, and the spirits residing within the balete trees, rivers, and mountains. When treated with respect, offerings of rice wine and betel nut, they guard our crops. But when forgotten or left unburied after disaster, their grief curdles into malice, transforming them into restless Anito-Aswang that haunt the midnight fogs.*


#### The Shadow of Bakunawa
*Deep beneath the sea trenches slumbers the Bakunawa, the giant serpent of the deep whose hunger once consumed six of the seven original moons. The ancestors did not destroy the creature; they lulled it to sleep using the synchronized rhythm of brass gongs and the shared memory of the living. Should the song of memory ever stop, the serpent stirs, and its breath rises as a tempest that swallows islands whole.*


#### The Custom of Barter and Handumanan
*In our islands, wealth is not measured by coin, for gold cannot be eaten when the monsoon rages. True wealth lies in Handumanan—remembrance held within heirloom beads, brass gongs, and woven fabrics. When two barangays exchange goods, they do not merely trade rice for fish; they exchange tokens of mutual reliance that bind their ancestors together.*


#### The Way of the Babaylan
*The Babaylan is the bridge between the seen world of fields and boats and the unseen world of spirits. Through the trance chant (Pag-anito) and dance accompanied by small flat gongs, she steps into the spirit realm to negotiate health for the sick and rain for the rice paddies. A barangay without a Babaylan is like a boat without a rudder in open waters.*

## ⚙️ Systems Design


### Core Gameplay Loop

Designed for a 10-minute mobile session structured into four phases: 1) Morning Check (1.5 min): Harvest crops from paddy and swidden plots, collect coconut tuba sap, and inspect coastal fish traps; 2) Island Expedition & Trade (4 min): Set sail on an outrigger proa canoe to a neighboring island or reef, engaging in barter trade, hunting, or gathering; 3) Rebuilding & Crafting (2.5 min): Use collected rattan, timber, and fibers to craft items or contribute to communal village restoration (bayanihan); 4) Evening Rest & Trust Ritual (2 min): Gift surplus commodities to the communal pantry or village chieftain, boosting Village Trust before resting to refresh stamina for the next day cycle.


### Farming & Hunter-Gathering

Employs a dual plot system reflecting authentic precolonial island agriculture: Wet Paddy (Sawah) for water-intensive crops like Palay, requiring monsoon rains or manual irrigation; and Dry Swidden (Kaingin) for drought-tolerant roots like Kamote and Gabi. Soil health is managed through crop rotation and fish offal composting. Weather drives hydration—the Habagat monsoon automates watering, whereas the Amihan dry season demands bamboo irrigation work. Growth timers match session pacing, allowing crops to mature across 1-3 play sessions.


### Trading System

A pure barter economy operating on relative value matrices rather than monetary currency. Merchants evaluate offers based on regional scarcity, utility, and seasonal demand—for example, coastal settlements overvalue timber and grains, while inland river communities overvalue salt, fish, and sea oils. The trading interface uses a visual balance scale that weighs offered goods against requested inventory. Players can perform haggling ('Tawad') or offer bonus ceremonial gifts to tip trade scales favorably.


### Day/Night Cycle & Weather

A 10-minute real-time day cycle divided into Morning (2 min - farming & trap prep), Midday (4 min - island voyages, foraging, bartering), Evening (2 min - crafting, communal dining, trust gifting), and Night (2 min - shelter fortification, storm warnings, stamina sleep). The climate transitions every 7 in-game days across three seasons: Amihan (Northeast Monsoon - clear skies, steady sailing), Habagat (Southwest Monsoon - heavy rains, boosted crop growth, turbulent seas), and Unos (Storm Season - devastating weather surges, isolated trade routes, emergency shelter preservation).


### Village Trust System

Village Trust is a collective socio-cultural metric ('Bayanihan System') that replaces individual monetary wealth. Players build Trust by depositing surplus food into the communal storehouse, rebuilding destroyed village dwellings, and gifting luxury items like Tuba or Agila during communal gatherings. Higher Trust tiers unlock community-assisted farming labor, access to shared village proa vessels, lower trade profit margins with allied islanders, and access to sacred crafting knowledge. Neglecting communal obligations or hoarding rare supplies during storm disasters triggers Trust decay, restricting progression until community reparations are made.

### Economy Balance Notes

Economy balance relies on organic decay sinks and dynamic seasonal demand spikes rather than gold sinks. Raw organic goods decay over time if unpreserved (e.g., fresh Bangus spoils into low-grade fertilizer within 2 days unless salted or smoked), encouraging constant resource rotation. Supply and demand fluctuate dramatically around the storm cycle: pre-Unos periods cause massive demand spikes for preserved foods (Kalamay, dried fish) and shelter timber, while post-Unos recovery periods double the trade value of building materials (Nipa fronds, Rattan). Sacred non-perishables like Mutya Pearls and Agila resin serve as stable high-value trade anchors.

## 🗺️ Level Design


### World Overview

SugiHandi is set within the fictive precolonial archipelago of Kulintra, a vast tropical network of sea-bound barangays, highland sanctuaries, and maritime trade ports shattered by a catastrophic super-typhoon known as the Great Sugi-Bagyo. The world reflects precolonial Southeast Asian history, animist metaphysics, and indigenous maritime culture. The player steps into the sandals of a solitary survivor who awakens among the mud and shattered timber of their home coastal settlement. Through non-verbal environmental cues, ancient Baybayin-inspired markings, ruined altars, and adrift trading artifacts, the game explores themes of ecological disaster, ancestral reverence, cultural memory, and quiet endurance across a storm-devastated tropical paradise.


### Map Topology

The Kulintra Archipelago is arranged in a non-linear hub-and-spoke oceanic topology. A central tempest sea basin, choked with treacherous coral reefs, sunken wreckage, and shifting fog banks, connects four distinct landmasses. Navigation relies on an upgraded traditional outrigger canoe (balangay). Ocean currents (Agas) and prevailing seasonal winds (Amihan and Habagat) dictate sea route accessibility. Shallow mangrove channels require lightweight bamboo rafts, while rough open-sea passages require deep-keeled balangay hulls fitted with woven abaca sails. Hidden sea passages (Lihim na Sabang) unlock as the player discovers ancient sea-charts, listens to wind-whistles at coastal shrines, or tides drop during dynamic lunar phases.


### Storm-Affected Zones

The Great Sugi-Bagyo leaves distinct ecological scars across each zone. High-impact windward shores feature stripped palm trees bent at unnatural angles, ash-gray soil profiles, and stranded sea life atop highland cliffs. River estuaries suffer from heavy siltation, turning once-vibrant blue lagoons into muddy channels filled with tangled forest debris. Conversely, low-lying volcanic zones exhibit stark contrasts between desolate obsidian fields and hyper-vibrant flora—glowing fungi, neon ferns, and wild orchids rapidly sprouting from nutrient-rich volcanic ash. Dynamic weather systems cycle through lingering drizzle, heavy tropical downpours, and brief bursts of piercing tropical sunlight, symbolizing the ongoing struggle and gradual healing of the natural world.


### Environmental Storytelling

Every location tells its story through spatial placement and visual artifacts rather than explicit text or NPC dialogue. Shattered balangay hulls angled toward open water show desperate escape attempts during the tempest. Terracotta burial jars broken open by mudslides reveal gold bead necklaces and brass armlets half-buried in river sand. Scorched woven banana leaves beside cold fire pits indicate rushed, unsuccessful ritual offerings to appease the sea spirits (Anito). Baybayin symbols carved into basalt cliffs mark safe freshwater springs or warn of dangerous shoals. In flooded trade posts, floating silk bolts intertwined with rotting nipa palm roofs convey the suddenness with which the typhoon destroyed centuries of built civilization.


### Islands


#### 🏝️ Isla Sugi — *Storm-Scarred Coastal Mangroves and Mudflat Settlement*
The starting island, once a thriving coastal barangay built on molave wood stilts over brackish estuaries. Now, it is a haunting landscape of splintered nipa huts, uprooted mangrove root systems, and ash-strewn mudflats swept by tide pools.

**Regions:**
- Sabang-Ili (River Mouth Settlement)
- Bakawan-Gulay (Tangled Salt-Mangrove Basin)
- Baybay-Abo (Ash-Swept Coral Shore)
- Bundok-Anito (Lowland Shrine Ridge)

**Points of Interest:**

- **Wreckage of the Datu's Balangay** (Player Hub & Workshop)
  The overturned hull of the village chieftain's flagship vessel, wedged between splintered palm trunks. Serves as the player's primary shelter, crafting station, and rest point.
  *Unlock: Unlocked automatically during the prologue.*

- **Scorched Pag-anito Shrine** (Ritual Site & Blessing Shrine)
  A stone pedestal surrounded by burned woven rattan mats, cracked terracotta jars, and calcified palm offerings dedicated to the ancestral water spirits.
  *Unlock: Requires crafting a basic obsidian knife to cut through dense tangled storm debris.*

- **Sabang Silt Pier** (Nautical Launch Site)
  A partially collapsed bamboo jetty extending into calm coastal waters, housing a repairable outrigger canoe hull.
  *Unlock: Requires gathering 5 resilient hardwood timbers and resin from Bakawan-Gulay.*

**Narrative Role:** Serves as the emotional anchor and tutorial zone, establishing the tragedy of the storm, basic survival crafting, and the player's motivation to search for surviving inter-island communities.


#### 🏝️ Mait-Estero — *Silted Trade Atoll and Submerged Rice Terraces*
A flooded low-lying archipelago that was once the economic heartbeat of maritime trade in Kulintra. Submerged market stilts, floating celadon pottery, and choked silt channels now lie beneath calm, silt-laden turquoise waters.

**Regions:**
- Lawa-Guinto (Golden Silt Lagoon)
- Kabayan-Luma (Foreign Trade Outpost)
- Lusong-Palay (Inundated Terrace Slopes)
- Karagatan-Lihim (Reef Ship Graveyard)

**Points of Interest:**

- **Guildhouse of the Chinese Trader** (Artifact Vault)
  A partially submerged two-story wooden warehouse containing waterlogged trade manifests, strings of copper cash coins, and intact Song-dynasty celadon jars.
  *Unlock: Requires obtaining the Bone Navigation Compass from Isla Sugi.*

- **Submerged Baylan Sluice Gate** (Environmental Puzzle)
  An ancient stone water gate adorned with carved serpent figures (Bakunawa) that once regulated agricultural freshwater flow across the terraces.
  *Unlock: Requires acquiring the Bronze Lever from the Foreign Trade Outpost.*

- **Ironwood Lookout Tower** (Cartography Point)
  A towering watchtower built from rot-resistant ironwood, wrapped in strangler fig vines, offering a panoramic view of northern sea passages.
  *Unlock: Requires clearing a nest of venomous sea snakes with smoke incense.*

**Narrative Role:** Introduces the broader world context of foreign maritime trade, wealth inequality before the disaster, and the failure of commercial power against nature's fury.


#### 🏝️ Dambana-Raja — *Highland Cloud Forest and Sacred Limestone Ravines*
A majestic mountainous island soaring above the storm fog. Pristine cloud forests, cascading waterfalls, and ancient megalithic burial grounds remain intact, preserved by sacred mountain geography.

**Regions:**
- Gubat-Amihan (Windward Cloud Jungle)
- Kabuatan-Lalim (Deep Waterfall Chasm)
- Tuktok-Anito (High Peak Spirit Sanctuary)
- Banal-Guinto (Hidden Gold-Vein Ridge)

**Points of Interest:**

- **Sanctuary of the Golden Likha** (Lore Keep & Altar)
  A cavernous limestone cathedral holding dozens of carved wooden idols (Likha/Tao-tao) with mother-of-pearl inset eyes, guarding gold funerary masks.
  *Unlock: Requires the Cloud Sanctuary Seal from the Baylan hermit of Mait-Estero.*

- **Chasm Rope Bridge Ruins** (Traversal Challenge)
  The frayed remains of a suspended woven abaca bridge stretching over a mist-shrouded 200-foot ravine.
  *Unlock: Requires gathering 4 bundles of braided high-altitude rope.*

- **Linao-Bagsak Purification Pool** (Restoration Site)
  A glowing crystal-clear spring where natural mineral waters purge exhaustion and empower the player's sea-blessing abilities.
  *Unlock: Requires offering 3 pristine orchids found in Gubat-Amihan.*

**Narrative Role:** Shifts the narrative focus from physical survival to spiritual recovery, ancestral wisdom, and learning the true mythological cause of the great storm.


#### 🏝️ Pulau Bato-Abo — *Volcanic Ash Wasteland and Obsidian Caldera*
The epicenter of the geological and meteorological catastrophe. A stark, dramatic island of jagged black obsidian glass, smoking fumaroles, scorched ironwood stumps, and glowing lava tube caves.

**Regions:**
- Baybay-Uling (Charcoal Coastline)
- Lawa-Kintab (Obsidian Glass Ravine)
- Baitang-Apoy (Active Caldera Rim)
- Gubat-Sunog (Scorched Basalt Forest)

**Points of Interest:**

- **The Great Geothermal Smelter** (Endgame Forge)
  An ancient megalithic furnace built directly over a volcanic thermal vent, used by precolonial metallurgists to forge sacred bronze cannons (lantaka) and meteorite blades.
  *Unlock: Requires wearing the Flame-Resistant Baylan Woven Cloak.*

- **Dolmen Tomb of the First Rajah** (Ancient Dungeon / Narrative Peak)
  A massive stone dolmen buried beneath volcanic tephra, housing the ancestral lineage relics, gold breastplates, and the final historical tablet of Kulintra.
  *Unlock: Requires assembling four elemental carved stone discs gathered across all islands.*

- **Caldera Ridge Summit** (Epilogue Viewpoint)
  The highest cliff edge in the archipelago, where the storm clouds finally break to reveal sunlight streaming over the slowly rebuilding island world.
  *Unlock: Requires completing the Ritual of Renewal at the First Rajah's Tomb.*

**Narrative Role:** Climax of the journey. The player uncovers the dual nature of destruction and rebirth, using the volcanic ash as fertilizer for the archipelago's future revival.


### Exploration Progression

Progression in SugiHandi is tied directly to the player's outrigger boat capabilities, tide levels, and survival gear. The game starts on Isla Sugi, where the player learns movement, gathering, and basic tool crafting. Repairing the balangay's primary outrigger allows travel across the calm inner lagoon to Mait-Estero. In Mait-Estero, acquiring trade tools, heavy ironwood planks, and nautical charts enables the player to reinforce the boat's hull to navigate the rough open-sea currents protecting Dambana-Raja. Reaching the high peaks of Dambana-Raja yields sacred blessings, spirit charms, and heat-resistant weaves required to endure the noxious gases and extreme terrain of Pulau Bato-Abo. Each unlocked island rewards the player with unique materials, expanding their navigational radius and uncovering deeper layers of the world's tragic lore.

## 🎨 Art Direction


### Art Style

2D hand-painted illustrative art style combining expressive gouache textures with bold ink linework inspired by precolonial Visayan Baybayin calligraphy and Malay songket geometry. High-contrast silhouette layering reminiscent of traditional Wayang shadow puppetry meets atmospheric watercolor wash technique. Art direction prioritizes tactile digital-craft textures—raw bark cloth (tapa), woven rattan fibers, and oxidized gold leaf accents—optimized for high-contrast mobile OLED screens with deep blacks and vibrant jewel-toned pigments.


### Visual Inspirations

- Spiritfarer (Thunder Lotus Games) - Soft illustrative hand-drawn linework and emotional color staging
- Gris (Nomada Studio) - Fluid atmospheric watercolor washes and expressive architectural silhouettes
- Hades (Supergiant Games) - High-contrast rim lighting, graphic ink shadows, and dynamic character cut-ins
- Oxenfree (Night School Studio) - Environmental sense of scale, moody color gradients, and atmospheric lighting

### Character Design

Characters feature strong graphic silhouettes grounded in precolonial Southeast Asian attire and adornments. Clothing includes draped hand-woven malong, patterned bahag loincloths, woven rattan salakot headwear, and heavy gold ornaments (kamagi chains and calombiga armlets). Skin textures incorporate authentic Visayan batok geometric tattoos inked directly into character line art, serving as narrative markers of lineage and spirit affinity. Fabric textures display visible warp-and-weft weave patterns with songket metallic brocade trims. Silhouettes avoid Western medieval tropes or generic anime proportions, favoring earthy, grounded human anatomical proportions with exaggerated gesture lines.


### Environment Design

Environments feature multi-layered 2D parallax scenes emphasizing vertical depth for portrait orientation (1080x1920). Architecture showcases stilt-supported torogan and nipa structures built from bamboo, coconut timber, and thatch roofing, heavily distressed by storm forces (snapped rattan lashings, splintered teak beams). Foliage transitions seamlessly from vibrant Monstera, banana leaves, and banyan roots to mud-splattered, defoliated wreckage. Dynamic environmental props—wind-blown inked tapa banners, floating river offerings, and glowing spirit lamps—add visceral life. Foreground visual framing uses dark silhouette leaves and carved okir wooden pillars to establish depth of field.


### UI Aesthetic

The user interface is designed as a physical, touchable artifact crafted from raw materials of precolonial island life. Windows and panels resemble aged, fiber-rich tapa cloth stretched across carved dark teak framing. Decorative borders incorporate carved Maranao okir scrollwork and Visayan geometric tattoo motifs. Buttons and iconography look like hand-carved bone, mother-of-pearl, and hammered brass. Baybayin script brushwork serves as subtle visual iconography and flourish elements alongside clear modern typography. Toggle states illuminate with ancestral gold leaf sheen and bioluminescent spirit glow, optimized for OLED contrast.


### Animation Style

Framerate-conscious 2D frame-by-frame animation mixed with skeletal cutout animation in Unity (using 2D Animation package). Motion features organic secondary physics—swaying cloth tassels, wind-whipped malong folds, hair flowing like tropical tide currents, and leaves drifting through heavy air. Keyframes emphasize weight, snap, and fluid weight transfers, drawing inspiration from traditional SEA dance movements (Pangalay and Singkil) and Wayang Kulit shadow puppet joints. Fluid spirit effects employ hand-drawn watercolor bleed transitions.


### Lighting Guidelines

Lighting relies on dramatic chiaroscuro with high color contrast optimized for OLED displays. Dark areas feature deep, rich blacks (#0B0D12) rather than washed-out grays. Key light sources—piercing tropical sunlight, glowing embers, bioluminescent spirit flora, and ancestral gold artifacts—cast intense colored rim lighting across character silhouettes and atmospheric fog layers. Volumetric light shafts ('god rays') cut through humid storm air using additive alpha blend shaders, casting soft ambient gradient bounces onto wet timber and foliage surfaces.


### ⚠️ Cultural Accuracy Notes

> STRICT CULTURAL CONTROLS - DO NOT: 1) Conflate precolonial Philippine/Malay visual culture with post-colonial Spanish era attire (strictly prohibit Spanish Barong Tagalog, Maria Clara dresses, or 19th-century colonial combs). 2) Use generic Polynesian or Maori tribal tattoo patterns (Koru, Marquesan crosses) instead of authentic Visayan batok or Northern Luzon Kalinga geometric iconography. 3) Treat Baybayin script as decorative gibberish or flipped lettering—all script glyphs must be linguistically verified or used strictly as stylized abstract geometry. 4) Misappropriate sacred indigenous ritual motifs (such as specific Maranao Okir fern motifs reserved for royalty or sacred datus) as random floor decorations. 5) Default to 'pan-Asian' visual shorthand like Japanese torii gates, Chinese roof tiles, or Indian mandalas.

## 💰 Monetization Plan


### Revenue Model

Free-to-play base game with optional ethically-priced cosmetic items, audio-visual expansions, and narrative side-story DLC packs. Zero pay-to-win, zero energy systems, and zero predatory pressure tactics.


### Ethical Principles

- Strict Zero Pay-to-Win: All monetization is strictly limited to cosmetic customization, soundtracks, and side-story narrative expansions; no gameplay advantage can ever be purchased.
- Cultural Preservation & Protection: Sacred, spiritual, and core historical cultural traditions are integrated authentically into the base game for all players and will never be paywalled or commercialized.
- No Artificial Friction or Timers: The game never sells energy refills, speed-ups, inventory expansion fees, or time-gated skips, maintaining a calm, respect-first gameplay loop.
- Transparent & Fair Pricing: All items are sold as direct-purchase microtransactions with clear real-world dollar prices. No obscure premium currency conversions or loot boxes.


### Ads Policy

Zero mandatory or intrusive pop-up ads. Optional rewarded video ads are strictly prohibited to protect immersive narrative flow and respect player attention.


### Battle Pass

A traditional battle pass is NOT recommended due to FOMO mechanisms and daily grind pressure. If introduced, it must be a permanent, non-expiring 'Seafarer's Chronicle' progression path where players can unlock rewards at their own pace without time limits or paid skips.
